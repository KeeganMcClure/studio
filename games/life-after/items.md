# Life After item list

Part of the Life After bible (`bible.md`, Items and crafting). Set Nov 21 from Keegan's review of the first item draft. It covers the first playable slice, survive a week alone, and grows from here.

- Most items take 1 slot. A few big ones take 2.
- Brand names are made up and only styled after the 1990s (see Setting).
- Numbers are on purpose left loose. This list settles what exists and what each thing is for. Exact numbers (damage, wear, spoil days, slots, storage size) go in spec 001 and get tuned in playtests.

## Farming and animals

Slow, free food that ties you to the hideout. The stake: it feeds you, but it needs daily work, makes noise, and gives raiders a reason to come.

- **Garden:** build a plot at the hideout and plant seed packets. Spring and summer grow, fall is the last harvest, winter grows nothing. A crop is ready after a few days; skip the daily watering and it fails. You can also grow weed from seeds found in a grower's stash (see Vices).
- **Hens:** found on farms and carried home. They need a coop and scraps each day, and each hen lays eggs daily. Clucking adds a little noise at the hideout.
- **Goats:** rarer. They need a pen and feed, and give milk daily. You can butcher one in a pinch, which costs SAN.
- **Fishing and snares:** a rod at the lake, or snares set in the woods and checked the next day, for raw meat.
- **The catch:** raiders want your animals and crops, and an attack can kill them. Moving hideout means leaving the garden behind (animals can come with you).

**Taking from the dead.** A dead survivor or raider drops what they carried: their bag and what's in it, a worn weapon, a few rounds, some food, and maybe a note or keepsake. Raiders carry more than loners. Searching a body takes time, and killing a person costs SAN. You can harden to it: after enough kills of people, it stops costing SAN. The stake is your companions. Kind ones trust you less, some evening talks close, and a few may leave.

## Bags

You start with pockets only (4 slots). Wearing a bag adds slots; you can wear one bag.

**Everyone carries.** Each companion has their own 4 pockets and can wear one bag. On the bag screen, L/R flips between people, and you can hand items across.

| Bag | Extra slots | Catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fanny pack | +2 | None |
| School backpack | +6 | None |
| Duffel bag | +8 | Slows you a little |
| Hiking pack | +10 | Rare; farm belt, campground |

**Hideout storage.** What you leave at the hideout goes in storage you build:

- **Crates and shelves:** more slots for each one built.
- **Root cellar:** food spoils slower inside.
- **Lockbox:** small, but raiders can't take what's in it.

Anything outside a lockbox can be stolen in a raid. How many slots each holds gets set in playtests.

## Crafting parts and first recipes

Parts: **scrap wood, duct tape, rope, rags, glass bottles, gasoline, tin cans.**

| Recipe | Needs | Makes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nail bat | Bat + nails | A stronger melee weapon |
| Board-up | Scrap wood + nails + hammer | Blocks a door or window |
| Weapon fix | Duct tape | Restores some wear on a melee weapon |
| Can alarm | Tin cans + rope | Rattles when something walks into it |
| Torch | Rags + scrap wood + lighter | Light that sees far; a zombie is stunned for 1 turn the first time it sees the flame; burns out |
| Firebomb | Glass bottle + gasoline + rags | Fire trap or a thrown fire; very loud |
| Rag bandage | Rags + clean water | A weaker bandage |

**How crafting works.** Your survivor keeps a notebook, and its Recipes page is the recipe book.

- **Open it from the bag menu.** It lists the recipes you know. One you can make now is lit; one you can't shows what's missing.
- **Learn recipes three ways:** a few to start, set by your job (a handyman knows the board-up). You can also read finds like how-to magazines and the field guide, or have a companion teach you at an evening talk.
- **Where you craft:** small things anywhere. Big ones (board-ups, the coop, storage) need a workbench at the hideout.
- **The stake:** crafting takes time on the clock, so a long build can run you into night.

## Keepsakes and special finds

Keepsakes do nothing in a fight. Finding one lifts SAN, and showing one to a companion at an evening talk can lift bond.

- **Mixtape** (a cassette with a hand-written label): plays on a radio corner; big SAN lift at the hideout.
- **Family photo**: a small lift, and sometimes a clue for a companion's arc.
- **Kid's drawing**: a small lift, and it hits harder for a parent.
- **Car keys**: start one specific car (needs gas and a battery). The key's tag names the car, like "blue Ridgeline wagon", and the car is always parked close by: the owner's driveway, or the lot by where you found them. Once you have the keys, the map marks it.

## 1990s finds

Picked by Keegan, Nov 21. Not picked: the disposable camera and the pager.

- **Cassette player (Soundwave):** SAN on the go; eats AA batteries, and you hear less while it plays.
- **Boombox:** set it playing to lure zombies away from where you're going; loud, uses D batteries.
- **Handheld game (Pocket Pixel):** small SAN lift; uses AA batteries.
- **CB radio:** talk to other survivors and hear rumors; raiders can listen too.
- **Instant camera:** a photo of a companion comes out right away and becomes a keepsake; film is rare.
- **VHS tapes:** watch one on a TV with power for a big SAN lift.
- **Sports cards:** worthless now, but some survivors trade well for them.
